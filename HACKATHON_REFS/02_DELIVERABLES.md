## Deliverable 2: Functional MVP (Scored Live at Launchpad)

**Must be:** An original, working prototype demonstrating core value of your AI-powered solution.

From handbook (verbatim):
> "Your functional MVP must be an original, working prototype that demonstrates the core value of your AI-powered solution. It does not need every future feature. It does need to work well enough for the jury to understand the intended user experience and evaluate the role of AI."

### What the MVP Must Make Visible (Verbatim from Handbook)

- **User:** Who is using the solution, and in what situation?
- **Need:** What specific executive function pattern or transition are you addressing?
- **Interaction:** What does the user do, and what does the system do next?
- **AI role:** What data or input informs the system, what does the model produce, and why is AI useful here?
- **Action:** How does the output help the user make a decision, take a step, or access support?
- **Learning:** What changed because of research or testing?

### Demo Readiness Requirements (Verbatim from Handbook)

> "Prepare a short primary demo path that works within the five-minute pitch. Use realistic sample data that does not expose private information. Test on the device, browser, network, and display setup you expect to use. Prepare a pre-recorded version of the demo. This is strongly recommended in case a live demonstration is not possible. Assign one person to operate the demo and one person to narrate what matters."

### What to Avoid (Verbatim)

> "Avoid the generic focus app. A timer, reminder list, or task tracker is not enough on its own. Show a distinct, research-informed AI capability and a clear reason it matters in the user's real context."

## What Jurors Need to Understand (Verbatim from Handbook)

1. The need is real, specific, and supported by Challenge Partner research.
2. The AI capability is purposeful, feasible, and meaningfully different from a generic productivity tool.
3. The MVP works and demonstrates the proposed value.
4. The team has considered ethics, privacy, accessibility, user agency, and technical limits.
5. The venture has a credible audience, adoption path, and potential to scale responsibly.

## Responsible AI Checklist (Verbatim from Handbook)

| Your Team Can Explain | The Question |
|---|---|
| Purpose | The specific benefit AI enables and why a simpler approach is not enough. |
| Agency | How users understand, control, correct, dismiss, or opt out of system suggestions. |
| Privacy | What data is needed, what is not collected, and how sensitive information is protected. |
| Bias and limits | Where outputs may be incomplete or wrong and how the experience reduces harm. |
| Accessibility | How the interface supports varied communication, sensory, cognitive, and motor needs. |
| Human support | When the system should defer to a person, service, educator, or qualified professional. |
