# North American AI Challenge 2026 — Complete Overview

**Full name:** North American AI Challenge for Neurodiverse Learning  
**Hosted by:** Spark Center for Innovation in Learning (SCIL) + ASU Enterprise Technology  
**Dates:** October 24 – November 21, 2026  
**Website:** tech.asu.edu/na-ai-challenge  
**Format:** Hybrid (virtual kickoff + in-person Launchpad pitches + in-person Grand Finale)

---

## About the Challenge (Verbatim from Slides)

> "From Oct. 24 to Nov. 21, the North American AI Challenge invites student teams, higher education institutions and service-minded organizations from across North America to advance AI for neurodiverse learning. This accelerated design and venture challenge invites students to prototype and pitch AI-driven ventures that support neurodivergent young people, while engaging higher education institutions and service-minded organizations across North America as partners and collaborators."

**Three defining pillars:**

1. **Customer Research** — "A core requirement of the Challenge is direct customer and market research. Teams must conduct customer research through a Challenge Partner, a curated list of institutions, programs, or service providers supporting neurodivergent individuals, ensuring proposals are grounded in real user needs."

2. **Hub-and-Spoke Model** — "Our Launchpad model operates as a regional hub-and-spoke designed to ensure research integrity, equitable access, and high-quality live competition experiences. Participation is designed to prioritize in-person engagement and verified customer research."

3. **Student Sinema Prize** — "Powered by real-world customer research conducted with vetted neurodiversity-serving partners, student teams will prototype and pitch venture-ready AI solutions through a competitive regional Launchpad model, with top teams advancing to a live grand finale in Phoenix, Arizona for the $15,000 Sinema Student Prize."

---

## The Challenge Statement (Verbatim)

> "Work as a team to build intelligent systems that decode 'executive function' — the mental skills we use to plan, focus, and juggle tasks — to better support individuals with ADHD, autism, dyslexia, and other neurodiversity, informed by real-world customer research and support from a Challenge Partner. Teams will prototype and pitch their solutions live to a jury panel at a regional Launchpad event, using a required 5-slide, 5-minute presentation template. Advancing teams will compete for the Sinema Student Prize in a final round of live pitches on Nov. 21 in Phoenix, USA."

**What executive function means here (from Handbook):**
> "Executive function includes the mental skills people use to plan, focus, remember steps, shift between tasks, regulate effort, and manage daily transitions. Your solution should respond to a specific need identified through research instead of assuming one experience fits everyone."

---

## What Will Make a Solution Competitive (From Handbook, Verbatim)

- A specific, research-supported problem instead of a broad assumption
- A novel and meaningful use of AI, not AI added as decoration
- A working functional minimum viable prototype
- Clear evidence that Challenge Partner research influenced the design
- Responsible handling of privacy, bias, accessibility, and user agency
- A credible path toward adoption, sustainability, and scale

---

## What Teams Will Do (Verbatim from Slides)

> "In this competition, student teams will:
> - Conduct direct partner research — Engage with an assigned Challenge Partner (a vetted institution, program, or service provider) to ground proposals in lived experience, institutional realities, and real user needs.
> - Build an original, working functional Minimum Viable Prototype (FMVP) that incorporates AI and partner research within the intensive two-week design sprint.
> - Pitch live in-person at a regional Challenge Launchpad by Nov. 7 to earn a spot at the live grand finale on Nov. 21 in Phoenix, Arizona to compete for the $15,000 Sinema Grand Prizes and seed funding, as well as global showcase opportunities."

---

## Team Requirements

**From slides:**
- All teams must be pre-formed and are composed of **3–5 teammates**
- All teammates must submit individual registrations by Oct. 7 to be included in the challenge
- Teams are required to incorporate customer research data and/or strategies through a Challenge Partner
- Teams are required to create a functional minimum viable prototype (FMVP) that incorporates AI and customer research
- FMVP submissions must be original work completed within the 2-week challenge sprint
- No extensions will be granted
- Teams agree to pitch live at a regional Launchpad by Nov. 7; at least 2 members must be present
- Submissions will undergo a code review

**From handbook (additional):**
- Every team member must be currently enrolled at a higher education institution in the United States or Mexico
- Each team may apply to and participate through only one regional Launchpad hub

**Team profile desired (from Team Deck verbatim):**
> "We are seeking mature teams of 3–5 students from eligible North American universities—specifically interdisciplinary groups of developers, entrepreneurs, and neurodiversity advocates who possess established trust and a proven collaborative history. While you bring your existing team chemistry, we challenge you to build a completely original, AI-powered venture from scratch during our intensive 2-week sprint. Your mission is to develop a functional Minimum Viable Prototype (MVP) that centers on a crucial customer experience, transforming your collective expertise into a brand-new solution for the neurodivergent community."

## ASU Charter (Foundational Context)

> "ASU is a comprehensive public research university, measured not by whom it excludes, but by whom it includes and how they succeed; advancing research and discovery of public value; and assuming fundamental responsibility for the economic, social, cultural and overall health of the communities it serves."

**ASU's 9 Design Aspirations:**
1. **Leverage our place** — ASU embraces its cultural, socioeconomic and physical setting.
2. **Fuse intellectual disciplines** — ASU creates knowledge by transcending academic disciplines.
3. **Be socially embedded** — ASU connects with communities through mutually beneficial partnerships.
4. **Transform society** — ASU catalyzes social change by being connected to social needs.
5. **Conduct use-inspired research** — ASU research has purpose and impact.
6. **Engage globally** — ASU engages with people and issues locally, nationally and internationally.
7. **Value entrepreneurship** — ASU uses its knowledge and encourages innovation.
8. **Enable student success** — ASU is committed to the success of each unique student.
9. **Practice Principled Innovation** — ASU places character and values at the center of decisions and actions.
