## Mission & Vision (Verbatim)

**Mission:**
> "The mission of the Arizona Autism Charter Schools, Inc. is to educate students with autism and other neurodiversities using evidence-based strategies grounded in the principles of Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA). This educational model will be delivered by highly trained teachers and staff. Our goal is to make this high quality, specialized education accessible to students with autism in Arizona and beyond."

**Vision:**
> "Our students will be empowered to reach their full potential in motivating, compassionate environments using individualized programs so that every child gains the skills to integrate into the community and become a fulfilled, productive citizen. Furthermore, we will continue to support our students with wraparound services and continued programming by developing a lifespan of supports."

**Future Vision:**
> "AZACS vision is to be Arizona's premier high-quality, tuition-free provider of seamless autism programming from childhood to adulthood, meeting the growing demand across the State and the increasing need to serve our K-12 graduates in specialized workforce development programs. This will give autism parents peace of mind that there will be a lifetime of quality education and programming, with no expiration date."

---

## Who AZACS Serves (Verbatim)

> "AZACS is Arizona's first and only charter network specializing in serving students on the autism spectrum. Our schools are tuition-free and do not require any payments or scholarships. We serve grades K-12+ and have several campuses across AZ to meet family and student needs. We are an open-enrollment public charter school, so we serve all levels of the autism spectrum as well as students with all learning styles."

## Core School Model (Verbatim)

**Four pillars:**
1. **Programs that Address the Spectrum** — Three programs address diversity in students' needs: Academic, Modified Academic, and Functional Academic Programs
2. **Small class sizes** — Class sizes range from 8 to 14 students, with an average 3:1 student-to-staff ratio across the school
3. **Virtual model options** — Full-time virtual model available to students across all three academic program models
4. **Comprehensive services** — Classrooms are supported by instructional coaches, behavior specialists, and speech, occupational, and physical therapists

---

## Three Educational Programs (Verbatim Detail)

### Academic Program (~12–14 students)
**Academics:**
- Working at or near grade level, based on standardized assessment
- Qualifies to take regular state assessment
- Communication at or near typical range
- Ability to follow general education curriculum

**Functional Skills:**
- Ability to access learning in a whole group setting
- Ability to transition within the classroom setting independently
- Independent with age-appropriate daily living skills

---

### Modified Academic Program (~10–12 students)
**Academics:**
- May have scattered skills in specific academic areas, but demonstrates deficits in academic domains overall
- Moderate communication deficits
- Ability to follow modified and differentiated curriculum

**Functional Skills:**
- Ability to access learning in a small group setting, still requiring some 1:1 intervention
- Ability to transition within the classroom setting with prompting
- Approaching independence with hygiene and toileting at an age-appropriate level

---

### Functional Academic Program (~8–10 students)
**Academics:**
- Benefits from highly structured and ABA-enriched environment (i.e., clinic model)
- Significant intellectual, developmental, and communication deficits
- Likely qualifies for alternative state assessment
- Requires intensive intervention and alternative curriculum across academic domains

**Functional Skills:**
- Requires assistance and/or supervision with daily living skills
- Requires some 1:1 intervention and support during transitions
- Ability to access learning in small group and 1:1 setting

## Academic Benchmarks & Measurement (Verbatim)

> "Academic Goals: Growth orientation – Student Achievement goals are based on growth, not proficiency"

**iReady** — Academic and Modified Academic program students use iReady to measure ELA & Math growth

**PEAK Program (Promoting Emergence of Advanced Knowledge):**
> "Functional program students are taught using the PEAK Relational Training Program, an evidence-based assessment and corresponding individualized curriculum that combines the traditional ABA verbal behavior approach with the science of Derived Relational Responding. AZACS has used PEAK for six years and has significant data showing student growth across multiple years and students."

**Student Engagement Indicator (PLA-Checks):**
> "Students with autism can find it challenging to stay engaged with instruction; AZACS tracks student engagement using a 'Planned Activity Check' (PLA-Checks) to help decrease students' maladaptive behaviors and assist with skill acquisition."

**New Methods:**
> "AZACS introduced new methods that are pedagogically aligned and grounded in the principles of ABA (Applied Behavior Analysis) to evaluate student performance"

**Equitable Evaluation:**
> "Methods incorporate different evaluation and goals for students in its Academic and Modified Academic programs (more independent and able to access learning in whole group settings) and its Functional program (more severe cognitive disability)"

**Authorizer Approval:** Methods were approved by AZACS' authorizer, the Arizona State Board for Charter Schools (ASBCS)

## Complete AZACS Glossary (Verbatim from Slides)

### Staff Roles

**Campus Director:** The leader responsible for a school campus's daily operations, staff, and student programs. Similar to a principal, this person works with families and staff to support a safe and effective learning environment.

**Case Manager:** A staff member, typically a special education teacher, who coordinates a student's Individualized Education Program (IEP). This person helps organize meetings, monitor progress, and communicate with the family and staff about the student's services and goals.

**EPE Program Manager:** A person who oversees programming at Essential Piece Enterprises (EPE), AZACS's sister nonprofit supporting adults with disabilities. This role helps coordinate staff and services that support employment skills, independence, and participation in the community.

**Special Education Teacher:** A certified teacher who adapts instruction and provides specialized instruction and support based on students' individual learning needs and IEPs.

**General Education Teacher:** A teacher who provides instruction in academic subjects aligned with grade-level learning standards, adapting lessons to help students access the material.

**Behavior Specialist / Paraprofessional:** A staff member who works with teachers and specialists to help students participate in lessons, communicate, practice skills, and use individualized behavioral supports.

**Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA):** A credentialed and graduate-level professional who specializes in Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) and studies how learning and the environment influence behavior. BCBAs develop and monitor individualized strategies to teach skills and support students' participation and independence. They work to understand, shape, and improve human behavior.

**Related Services Provider:** A professional who provides specialized services a student needs to benefit from education. Examples include speech-language therapists, occupational therapists, and physical therapists.

**Job Coach:** A staff member who helps students or adults learn job tasks, practice workplace expectations, and become more independent at work.

**Director of STEAM and CTE:** A leader who oversees learning opportunities in science, technology, engineering, arts, and mathematics, along with programs that prepare students for careers.

---

### Student Programs and Supports

**Essential Piece Enterprises (EPE):** AZACS's sister nonprofit that provides adult programming, including opportunities to develop employment skills and greater independence.

**Individualized Education Program (IEP):** A written plan developed by a school team and the student's family for an eligible student with a disability. It identifies learning goals and the specialized instruction, services, and supports the student will receive.

**Special Education:** Instruction designed to meet the individual needs of a student with a disability. It can take place in different classroom settings.

**Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA):** An evidence-based approach to understanding how people learn and how their surroundings affect behavior. At school, ABA strategies can help teach communication, academic, social, and daily living skills.

**Neurodiversity / Neurodivergent:** Neurodiversity refers to the natural variety in how people think, learn, and process information. Neurodivergent describes people whose ways of processing information differ from what is considered typical, including many people with autism or ADHD.

**Academic, Modified Academic, and Functional Academic Programs:** AZACS instructional pathways that provide different levels and types of support. Depending on the program and the student's needs, instruction may emphasize grade-level academics, adapted academic content, or practical academic and daily living skills.

**Differentiation:** Adjusting how a lesson is taught, practiced, or demonstrated so students with different learning needs can participate and make progress.

**Accommodation:** A change in how a student accesses learning or shows understanding, such as extra time, visual directions, or a quieter workspace.

**Modification:** A change in what a student is expected to learn or demonstrate, based on the student's individual needs.

**Behavioral Supports:** Strategies that help students communicate their needs, participate in activities, and develop useful skills. Supports may include predictable routines, visual schedules, or teaching students how to request a break.

**Sensory Needs:** Individual differences in how a person experiences sound, light, touch, movement, and other sensations. Some students benefit from changes to the environment or access to sensory tools.

**Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC):** Ways to communicate that supplement or replace speech, such as pictures, gestures, communication boards, or speech-generating devices.

**Student Independence / Self-Management:** Skills that help students make choices, complete tasks, ask for support, and monitor their own progress with increasing independence.

---

### Learning and Careers

**STEAM:** Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts, and Mathematics. STEAM learning often combines these subjects through hands-on projects and problem-solving.

**Career and Technical Education (CTE):** Courses and learning experiences that teach skills related to specific careers and workplaces.

**Transition Services:** Activities and supports that prepare students for life after high school, including further education, employment, and independent living.

**Postsecondary:** Anything that occurs after high school, such as college, career training, employment, or adult services.

**Academic Growth:** The progress a student makes over time, compared with the student's earlier performance.

**Student Engagement:** A student's active participation in learning. Engagement can look different for each student and may include using a communication device, completing a task, or responding through movement or gestures.

**Progress Monitoring:** Regularly collecting information to see whether a student is learning and whether teaching strategies or supports need to change.
