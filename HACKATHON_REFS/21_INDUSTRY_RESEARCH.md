# Industry Research — Gap Analysis & Competitive Landscape
**Compiled: Oct 26 | Team Kaleidoscope**

> This document covers what currently exists across the four gap areas identified from First Place AZ research. For each area: who's in the market, what they do, what they miss, and where the white space is.

---

## Gap 1: Social Connection for Neurodiverse Adults

### The Problem (Validated by Research)
Autistic adults overwhelmingly report wanting friendships — but social initiation requires the exact EF and social cognition skills that are hardest for them. The service cliff (age 22) removes structured school-based social contexts. Isolation and loneliness rates in autistic adults are described as "unprecedented" (Josh, First Place kickoff).

---

### Who's In the Market

#### Synchrony *(launched February 2026 — 8 months old)*
- **What:** First-of-its-kind social app for neurodivergent adults. Dating + friendship.
- **How it works:** Interest-based matching. Verification and moderation for safety. Optional AI coach "Jesse" who offers prompts when users are stuck mid-conversation — interprets confusing social situations, suggests what to say next, protects boundaries.
- **Jesse's constraint:** Activates only when user prompts it. Cannot engage in any other type of interaction. Non-therapeutic positioning.
- **Target:** Adults 18+, neurodivergent broadly (autism, ADHD, etc.)
- **Funding:** Early-stage startup, 3 co-founders (one autism parent, one serial entrepreneur, one autism specialist)
- **Gap they leave:** General internet-wide matching. Not context-specific. Not built for a residential community where people already live together. No integration with existing activity infrastructure. No employment dimension.

#### Kaboose *(launched July 2026 — 3 months old)*
- **What:** Neurodivergent community + learning platform. Explicitly NOT dating.
- **How it works:** Interest groups, live events, "Neuro Nuggets" (bite-sized expert-led learning modules — video/audio/text). 135 countries. Free with premium.
- **Key design choice:** No dating, explicitly friendship/community/learning only — kept intentionally low-pressure.
- **B2B angle:** Also sells to employers as workplace neurodiversity support ("private by design — individual activity never reported to employers")
- **Gap they leave:** Global community, not local. No AI matching. No integration with residential community. No employment retention support.

#### Special Bridge
- Dating and friendship site for people with disabilities broadly (not ND-specific)
- Traditional matching model, no AI coaching

#### NDFriends *(indie, iOS, just building)*
- Reddit post from a developer building a friend-matching app for ND adults
- Very early stage, no significant traction

#### PEERS Program (UCLA)
- Research-backed social skills training curriculum, used in clinical settings
- In-person or provider-delivered — not a product you can access independently
- Synchrony explicitly references PEERS as the research base for their approach

---

### Noora — AI Social Coach *(Stanford HAI, academic research)*
- **What:** LLM-based chatbot teaching empathy and social communication skills to autistic adults
- **How it works:** Presents social scenarios → user responds → Noora grades and coaches. Empathy module: "I'm feeling really tired" → user responds → Noora gives feedback.
- **Research results (randomized trial, n=30):** 71% of participants improved empathetic responses. 38% improvement in real-life Zoom calls with a human after 4 weeks of use.
- **Critical finding:** The improvement generalized to real human interactions — not just performance in the app.
- **Status:** Seeking beta testers ages 11–35. Not a consumer product yet.
- **Gap they leave:** Practice tool, not support tool. Teaches skills in controlled scenarios; doesn't help in real moments of social interaction.

---

### What Nobody Is Doing

**The First Place opportunity is fundamentally different from all of the above:**

Every existing solution is built for the general ND population connecting over the internet. First Place's situation is:
- Residents already **live in the same building** — they don't need to find each other
- Activity infrastructure already exists (**LifeLoop + 1,600 activities/year**) — they already share experiences
- The gap is not finding people — it is **initiating and sustaining connections** with neighbors they already see

**The white space:** A context-aware social scaffold that uses shared activity history ("you both attended cooking class 3 times this month") to lower the EF barrier to initiating conversation — not a matching app, but a connection bridge within an existing community.

## Gap 2: Employment Retention for Neurodiverse Adults

### The Problem (Validated by Research + Josh)
Getting a job is hard. Keeping it is harder. First Place's Be Open Be Ready program achieves 50% employment — which is strong. But that means 50% are not retaining employment. Josh explicitly flagged "it's one thing to get a job, it's even harder to keep it." Georgia Tech researchers put a number on a key gap: government-contracted job coaches support for 90 days post-hire, but many companies have probationary periods exceeding 3 months. When the coach exits, the support falls off a cliff.

---

### Who's In the Market

#### Mentra
- **What:** Matching platform connecting neurodivergent job seekers with inclusive employers based on strengths, work preferences, and workplace needs (not just a resume)
- **Focus:** Job FINDING, not retention. Pre-hire.

#### Georgia Tech LLM Job Coaching Research *(NSF grant, Jan 2026)*
- **What:** $500,000 NSF grant to build LLMs for strength-based job coaching for autistic job seekers
- **Researchers:** Jennifer Kim (HCI) + Mark Riedl (AI/ML), Georgia Tech
- **Focus:** Interview prep, resume writing, cover letters, handling rejection, accommodation requests
- **Critical quote from researcher Heather Dicks:** *"I could see these models evolving to being supportive at those critical junctures of the probationary period being over, or the one-year job review, or the annual evaluation that everyone dreads."*
- **Status:** Still in research/development. **Not a product yet.** This is the closest thing to employment RETENTION AI — and it doesn't exist yet.

#### Inclusively
- **Product:** "Retain" — workforce intelligence platform helping employees find and use company benefits/support
- **Focus:** General workforce retention (not ND-specific), employer-side tool
- **Gap:** Not autism-specific. Not about on-the-job EF support. Benefits navigation, not workplace situation coaching.

#### EZducate AI
- Workplace social scripts, task analysis builders, ADA accommodation templates, job coaching data tools
- More of a therapist/job coach productivity tool than a resident-facing app

#### Daivergent
- Matches autistic people specifically to data-related jobs (data labeling, QA, etc.)
- Narrow focus on one job category. Job finding, not retention.

#### Dynseo / Job Coach Apps
- Digital tools to support job coaches managing clients
- Staff-facing, not resident-facing

---

### What Nobody Is Doing

**The specific gap for First Place:** In-the-moment, real-time support for the EF and social demands of keeping a job — not prep before the interview, not curriculum in a classroom, but:

- "My manager gave me feedback that felt unclear and I don't know how to respond"
- "The schedule changed unexpectedly and I'm overwhelmed"
- "I made a mistake at work and I don't know what to do"
- "I have my 90-day review tomorrow and I'm terrified"

Georgia Tech is building toward this but it's in research phase. No product on the market does this specifically. Combined with First Place's specific employer partner context (AMC Theaters, Phoenix Zoo, Aramark, Harkins), a solution could be employer-specific: the scripts and scenarios are tuned to those actual workplaces.

## Gap 3: Smart Home Data → Proactive Wellbeing

### The Problem (Observed by Anand)
First Place has smart building sensors in place — stove duration alerts, environmental monitoring. They are currently used **reactively** (alert fires when threshold exceeded). No one is using behavioral pattern data proactively to support resident wellbeing or detect early signs of isolation.

---

### Who's In the Market

#### Simply Home
- Smart home integration for residents with disabilities: lights, locks, stove sensors, panic pendants
- Hardware-focused safety system. Reactive alerts only.

#### Google Home / Amazon Alexa
- Generic smart home platforms, not disability-specific
- Reminders, timers, voice commands — no behavioral pattern analysis

#### HaloCare (UK-based)
- Appliance management sensors (oven, taps, kettle), activity pattern monitoring
- Primarily designed for elderly adults, not neurodiverse adults

#### Research-Level Systems (HABITAT, U-Health, RiSH)
- Academic smart home projects for elderly: motion sensors, bed sensors, wearables tracking vitals
- AI analyzes patterns, alerts caregivers if abnormalities detected
- All designed for elderly/health monitoring. None adapted for neurodiverse residential community.

#### University of Colorado Smart Home for IDD
- Smart home initiative for people with IDD
- Safety and monitoring focus. Resident-controlled? Unclear. Not commercially available.

---

### What Nobody Is Doing

**The insight:** All existing smart home research is safety-focused (fall detection, medication, wandering) and eldercare-oriented. Nobody has applied behavioral pattern data to **social wellbeing** in a neurodiverse residential community:

- "Resident hasn't been to shared spaces in 5 days (usually goes 3x/week)" → early isolation signal
- "Routine disruption pattern" → predict distress before it becomes a crisis
- Resident-facing: show the resident their own patterns ("You've been staying in your room more this week — would you like to see what's happening in the game room tonight?")

**Privacy architecture is the key design challenge:** This only works if the resident controls their own data and chooses what signals are visible to staff. Framed as resident empowerment, not surveillance. Dignity of risk applies.

## Gap 4: AI Social Skills Coaching (In-the-Moment Support)

### The Problem
Practice-based tools (Noora, social skills apps) teach skills in controlled scenarios. But real social situations — especially in the workplace — are unscripted, timed, and high-stakes. What autistic adults often need is not more practice but **real-time support** when they're in the middle of an actual interaction and don't know what to do.

---

### Who's In the Market

#### Noora (Stanford HAI)
- Practice-based: gives you scenarios, grades your responses, teaches empathy skills
- **Not** in-the-moment. You use it at home to practice, not during a real conversation.
- Research-validated (38% improvement in real human interactions after 4 weeks)

#### Chatterfly
- Conversation practice app for ND teens and adults
- AI coach pauses mid-conversation and offers prompts
- Closer to in-the-moment but still structured practice, not real-world deployment

#### TalkMaster AI / Social Skills Trainer (Google Play)
- Generic social skills practice, not ND-specific
- Practice environment only

#### Wysa
- AI-guided mental health app (CBT, emotional support, coping skills)
- Not ND-specific. Not social situation coaching. Mental health focus.

#### AI Chatbots (GPT, Claude, etc.) used informally
- Reddit/autism communities describe using ChatGPT to decode ambiguous social situations, draft responses, practice conversations
- Not structured, not validated, not designed for this use case — but it's happening organically

---

### What Nobody Is Doing

**The gap between "practice tool" and "in-the-moment support":**

Practice tools (Noora, Chatterfly) are valuable but separated from real situations. What's missing is a lightweight, on-demand tool that:

1. Helps decode ambiguous real situations ("my coworker said X — was that positive or negative?")
2. Suggests concrete responses without requiring the user to come up with one from scratch
3. Can be accessed discreetly during or after an interaction (not requiring a therapy session or practice routine)
4. Is designed specifically for the workplace context (not generic social scenarios)

This is the most buildable in 10 days — it's essentially a well-prompted LLM with a designed UX — but the differentiation comes from context-specificity and the First Place / employer partner angle.

## What This Means for Your Solution

### The Uniquely Defensible Position

Every general ND social app (Synchrony, Kaboose) is solving for people who don't know each other. First Place's residents already live together. **The connection problem is not finding people — it's bridging from shared activities to actual relationships.**

A solution that:
1. Sits on top of LifeLoop (or a parallel activity layer)
2. Recognizes shared activity attendance as a connection signal
3. Lowers the EF barrier of initiating ("Marcus also went to cooking class twice this month — here are 3 easy ways to say hi")
4. Optionally extends to employment moments ("you work at AMC — your manager said X — here's what that usually means and how to respond")

...is doing something nobody else is doing, is grounded in First Place's specific reality, and scales to the 70K+ who don't have a First Place.

### The Pitch-Ready Framing

> "Synchrony and Kaboose are solving internet-scale ND social connection. First Place residents don't need to find each other — they live in the same building. They need a bridge from shared experiences to actual friendships. That's the gap we're building for."
