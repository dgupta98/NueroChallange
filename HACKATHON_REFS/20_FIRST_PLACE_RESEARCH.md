## The Single Most Important Discovery

**First Place already uses LifeLoop** — an activity management platform built originally for senior living communities — for resident engagement. Residents sign up for activities on it. Families can see what their loved ones attended and view photos.

This means:
- "Build an engagement app" is partially solved — LifeLoop exists
- The gap is in what LifeLoop does NOT do
- Your solution must be meaningfully different from LifeLoop, not a replica of it

**Ask tomorrow:** How does LifeLoop actually work in practice? Do residents use it independently or only because staff prompt them?

## The Competitive Landscape — What Already Exists

### LifeLoop (Already in Use at First Place)
- **What it is:** Activity scheduling and family communication platform, originally built for senior living
- **What it does:** Activity sign-up, attendance tracking, family photo sharing, family updates
- **What it does NOT do:**
  - Peer-to-peer resident communication
  - Personalized activity recommendations
  - Employment support
  - Smart home data integration
  - Social matching between residents with similar interests
  - In-the-moment support ("I need something to do right now")

### NFlyte
- **What it is:** Life skills platform for autistic adults, designed for independent living
- **Features:** Visual schedules, shopping lists, community cookbook, mood check-ins, document storage
- **Caregiver dashboard:** Remote monitoring by family/support programs
- **Gap:** No AI personalization, no social matching, no employment retention, caregiver-centric (not resident-centric)
- **Status:** Website currently in maintenance mode — small company

### Simply Home
- Smart home integration for residents with disabilities (lights, locks, stove sensors, panic pendants)
- Hardware-focused, not engagement or social

### Google Home / Amazon Alexa
- Used for reminders, shopping lists, calendar alerts
- Not autism-specific; not social; not employment-focused

### DailyBuddy
- Task management, reminders, relaxation tools
- General-purpose, not residential community specific

### What's Missing in Everything That Exists
Every existing tool is either:
- **Caregiver-centric** (designed for the family/staff to monitor the adult) — violates dignity of risk
- **Task/schedule focused** (visual schedules, reminders) — doesn't address social isolation
- **Non-personalized** (same experience for everyone) — ignores varied interests, support needs, communication styles
- **Not resident-to-resident** — built for staff→resident or family→resident, not peer→peer

## The Real Problem: What 1,600 Activities Can't Fix

First Place runs 4+ activities every single day. They have a beautiful building, smart systems, employer partners, and a vibrant community. And yet — Josh told the team at kickoff:

> "There is a level of isolation and loneliness that is faced in the neurodivergent population that is unprecedented. So when people come to First Place, the first thing they're looking for is to build relationships with people."

The infrastructure for engagement exists. The gap is in **genuine social connection** — knowing who to connect with, how to initiate, and sustaining relationships. This is an executive function + social cognition problem, not an activities calendar problem.

**Why autistic adults struggle specifically with this:**
- Social initiation requires reading implicit cues about who is open to connection — hard
- Knowing when to reach out, what to say, how often — no script exists
- Activities are shared experiences, but they don't automatically produce friendships
- Rejection sensitivity can be intense — one awkward interaction leads to withdrawal

## Four Solution Directions — Ranked by Opportunity

### Direction 1: Resident Social Matching + Connection Scaffold ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
**The gap LifeLoop doesn't fill**

- AI understands resident interests, communication preferences, social goals (from onboarding profile)
- Suggests 1–3 specific connections: "Marcus also likes PlayStation and has been to the cooking class twice. Here's an easy way to start a conversation with him."
- Reduces the EF load of social initiation — gives a script, a context, a low-stakes entry point
- Peer-to-peer, not staff-mediated — respects autonomy
- Builds on activities that already happen (LifeLoop) instead of replacing them

**Why this is strong:** Josh said friendships are what residents want most. Nobody is solving this. LifeLoop schedules events; it doesn't build relationships.

---

### Direction 2: Employment Retention Coach ⭐⭐⭐⭐
**The gap "Be Open Be Ready" doesn't fill**

- Getting the job: First Place has employer partners, job coaches, resume help — covered
- **Keeping the job: not covered**
- Real-time support for the EF demands of staying employed: handling ambiguous manager feedback, schedule changes, workplace social friction, post-shift emotional decompression
- "You received a message from your supervisor that felt unclear. Here are 3 ways to respond."
- 50% of Be Open Be Ready graduates are employed — what about the other 50%? What happened?

**Why this is strong:** Josh explicitly flagged this. Employer partners exist. The data (50% success rate) implies 50% aren't retaining employment.

---

### Direction 3: Smart Home Data → Resident Wellbeing Layer ⭐⭐⭐
**Building on existing infrastructure Anand saw**

- Stove sensor, environmental sensors already in place
- Currently used reactively (alarm goes off → someone responds)
- Proactive use: pattern changes in a resident's routine can signal disengagement, distress, or isolation before it becomes a crisis
- "Resident hasn't been to the game room in 5 days (usually goes 3x/week) — flag for Community Life team"
- Privacy design is essential: resident sees their own data; staff see aggregated/flagged signals only with consent

**Why this is strong:** The hardware exists. No one is using the data intelligently. Lowest infrastructure lift.

**Risk:** Privacy is extremely sensitive. Needs to be explicitly resident-controlled.

---

### Direction 4: Personalized Activity Recommendation Engine ⭐⭐
**Making 1,600 activities usable instead of overwhelming**

- 1,600 activities/year = choice overload for someone with EF and decision-making challenges
- AI narrows to "the 3 activities this week that match your interests, goals, and social comfort level"
- Connects to LifeLoop data (what you've attended, what you rated, who you went with)
- Nudges toward activities that overlap with residents you haven't connected with yet

**Why this is weaker:** LifeLoop already does sign-ups. This is an add-on to something that already works. Lower impact than Directions 1–2.

## What the Research Changed — Draft Statement

> "We came in thinking the gap was 'no engagement platform exists.' Research showed First Place already uses LifeLoop for activity scheduling. The real gap is in peer-to-peer social connection — residents have 1,600 activities but still report isolation and loneliness. Our solution changed from 'build an engagement app' to 'build the social initiation scaffold that turns shared activities into actual friendships.'"

## Tomorrow's Visit — What to Observe

**When you walk in:**
- Look at how common spaces are used — are people in them together or alone?
- Is LifeLoop visible anywhere (screens, posters)? Do residents reference it?
- Smart home infrastructure: notice where sensors are; how are they displayed to residents?
- Is there any resident-to-resident communication visible (bulletin boards, notes, group chats on TVs)?

**Physical spaces Anand saw — go deeper:**
- Game room: is it spontaneous or scheduled? Do residents go alone or together?
- Teaching kitchen: how are cooking activities organized? Who joins?
- Individual apartments: what communication tools are visible inside?

## Tomorrow's Visit — Key Questions to Ask

### On Social Connection (Priority 1)
1. How does a new resident typically make their first friend here? Walk me through what that looks like.
2. Are there residents who are isolated even with 1,600 activities available? What does that look like?
3. Is there any way residents currently communicate with each other asynchronously — group chat, bulletin board, app?
4. What's the hardest part of social connection for your residents specifically?

### On LifeLoop (Priority 2)
5. How do residents actually use LifeLoop — do they browse it themselves or does staff help them sign up?
6. What does LifeLoop not do that you wish it did?
7. Has anyone tried building resident-to-resident features? What happened?

### On Employment Retention (Priority 3)
8. When a resident gets a job and then loses it — what's the most common reason?
9. What support exists between getting hired and the 3-month mark (the hardest period)?
10. Is there any tool residents use when they hit a hard situation at work?

### On Smart Home Data (Priority 4)
11. Who currently sees the smart home sensor data? Is it just safety alerts or is there more?
12. Are there any plans to use the building's data to proactively support residents?
13. What would residents be comfortable with in terms of their data being used to support them?

### On the Population (Context)
14. What's the biggest misconception people have when they first come to First Place about what residents need?
15. What does "thriving" look like for a resident who has been here 2 years?

## What NOT to Build (Based on Research)

| Don't Build | Why |
|---|---|
| Another activity calendar / scheduler | LifeLoop already does this |
| A caregiver monitoring dashboard | Violates dignity of risk; residents are adults, not patients |
| A general task manager (like NFlyte) | Exists; doesn't address social isolation |
| Something requiring staff to set up or operate for residents | Dependency, not independence |
| A "facility management" tool | First Place is not a facility — cultural absolute |
| Something that only works inside First Place | Josh said build for the 70K+ who don't have access |
