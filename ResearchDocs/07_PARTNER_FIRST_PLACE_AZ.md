## Mission

Create a supportive, sustainable community where adults with autism and/or intellectual/developmental disabilities (A/I/DD) live and thrive with pride, purpose and endless possibilities.

Goal: ensure housing and community options are as bountiful for special populations as they are for everyone else.

---

## First Place–Phoenix (The Physical Community)

Broke ground December 2016, opened to residents July 2018.

### Three Primary Components

**1. First Place Apartments**
- 55 studio, one-, two-, and four-bedroom units
- Up to 67 residents with A/I/DD
- Access to various services and amenities

**2. First Place Transition Academy**
- Collaboration with SARRC (Southwest Autism Research & Resource Center)
- 2-year, 32-course life skills program
- Curriculum: **Learn4Independence®**
- Prepares students for employment and independent living

**3. First Place Global Leadership Institute (GLI)**
- International education and training center
- Faculty of experts focused on housing, healthcare, and independent living
- Serves: educators, researchers, support staff, medical professionals
- R&D site and public policy advancement hub

## Key Themes / Pain Points for Solution Design

**Population served:** Adults with autism and/or intellectual/developmental disabilities (not children)

**Core life challenges they address:**
- **Independent living** — How to live with appropriate support while maintaining autonomy and pride
- **Employment** — Accessing and sustaining meaningful work
- **Healthcare** — More inclusive healthcare; Creighton University MED 459 rotational elective program as an example
- **Transportation** — Partnership with Waymo (autonomous vehicles) to help residents access employment and community
- **Civic engagement** — "Vote the Spectrum" initiative to support civic participation
- **Social connection** — Community and peer relationships

**What success looks like to them:**
- Adults with A/I/DD living and thriving with "pride, purpose, and endless possibilities"
- Replicable models that can be adopted in other cities/regions
- Public policy advances that expand housing, healthcare, community options

---

## Why They're a Good Design Partner

- Real-world residential community — users are **adults living independently** (different from K–12 school context)
- Can surface transition challenges between school and adult life
- Focus on **life transitions** (school → work → living independently) — directly maps to executive function challenges
- Innovation partnerships already in place (Waymo, Creighton, SARRC) — open to technology
- Specific population: **adults, not children** — solutions must work for adult users

## Design Considerations for Solutions Targeting This Partner

- Users are **adults** — interface must be adult-appropriate (not childish)
- Many residents have significant support needs but also desire **autonomy and agency**
- Employment and daily transitions are high-stakes pain points
- Privacy is extremely important — no photos of residents
- Solutions that help with **scheduling, daily routines, job prep, communication** are high-relevance
- The community already has on-site support staff — solutions could augment staff, not replace them
- Scalability point: if replicable, could be adopted by similar residential communities nationally
