# Partner Research Guide — How to Conduct Required Customer Research

Source: 2026 NA AI Challenge Handbook (English)

---

## Why Partner Research Is Non-Negotiable

Partner research is not optional or a formality. It directly affects:
- Your score on **User-Centered Problem** (rubric criterion #2)
- Your score on **Impact & Inclusivity** (rubric criterion #3)
- Your score on **Principled Innovation** (rubric criterion #6)
- Your written responses (Partner Research & Insights section)
- Your disqualification risk if you skip it entirely

**From handbook:** "Your partner is a vetted institution, program, or service provider supporting neurodivergent people. Use this access to understand lived experience, organizational realities, and the conditions your solution must work within."

---

## Research Preparation (Before the Conversation) — Verbatim from Handbook

> "Choose a research goal and write the three most important things you need to learn. Assign a facilitator, note taker, timekeeper, and follow-up owner. Prepare open-ended questions that do not lead participants toward your idea. Decide what information you truly need and avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive data."

### Team Roles to Assign:
- **Facilitator** — runs the conversation
- **Note taker** — captures everything said
- **Timekeeper** — keeps the conversation on schedule
- **Follow-up owner** — handles thank you and any promised next steps

### Question Preparation Rules:
- Write open-ended questions (not "Would you use a task tracker?" — that leads toward your idea)
- Focus on: current behaviors, barriers, workarounds, transitions, support systems
- Do NOT collect sensitive data you don't need
- Write your 3 most important learning goals before every conversation

---

## During the Research Conversation — Verbatim from Handbook

> "Ask about current behaviors, barriers, workarounds, transitions, and support systems. Listen for differences across people and contexts; do not treat one story as universal. Ask what success, trust, control, and accessibility would look like to the people involved. Confirm what you may quote, summarize, or show in your presentation."

**Critical:** "Do not treat one story as universal." — You need multiple perspectives.

**Confirm permissions:** Before using any quotes or examples in your pitch, verify with the partner what you may quote, summarize, or show.

---

## After the Research Conversation — Verbatim from Handbook

> "Separate direct evidence from your team's interpretation. Group observations into patterns, tensions, needs, and design constraints. Write at least one clear statement describing how the research changed your solution. Thank the partner and close any promised follow-up."

### Four Categories to Sort Your Notes Into:
1. **Patterns** — things that came up repeatedly across people/contexts
2. **Tensions** — contradictions, tradeoffs, or competing needs
3. **Needs** — specific things people need that aren't being met
4. **Design constraints** — things your solution must work within (technical, institutional, user, environmental)

### Required Output:
**Write at least one statement:**
> "Our research changed our solution because _______________."

If you can't write this statement, you didn't conduct research with an open mind.

Schedule through the platform of preference for the Challenge Partner. Don't be late. When in doubt, @ Challenge Organizer on Discord for guidance."

## What Good Research Looks Like (from Handbook)

You need to understand:
- **Lived experience** — what it's actually like for neurodivergent individuals day-to-day
- **Organizational realities** — constraints, resources, systems, processes the partner operates within
- **Conditions your solution must work within** — technical, environmental, privacy, accessibility, staffing

Your solution must show:
- Clear evidence that Challenge Partner research **influenced the design**
- The need is **specific**, not broad
- The research **changed your thinking** (not confirmed your pre-existing idea)

## AZACS-Specific Research Angles (Based on Staff Pain Points)

### High-priority problems to explore in your AZACS meetings:

**1. Student self-tracking / independence (Gave Warrior, Kaleigh VanAlstine)**
- How do students currently track their own behavior and academic progress?
- What happens when you try to gradually release responsibility to them?
- What does "I don't need to do this" look like in practice? When does it happen?
- What would ideal student-owned tracking look like?

**2. Curriculum adaptation at speed (Dylan Shloss)**
- Walk me through a CKLA lesson. Where exactly does it break down for your students?
- What does a "modified version" look like when you make one quickly?
- How much time does modification currently take?
- If a tool generated modifications, what would make them trustworthy vs. not?

**3. Teacher workload and time (multiple staff)**
- What tasks consume the most time after school hours?
- What do you wish you could hand off?
- What would you be nervous about handing off to AI?

**4. Differentiation in real-time (Harpreet Kaur, Amelie Wachsmuth)**
- Describe a moment when you had to quickly identify what 3 different students needed simultaneously.
- What signals tell you a student is about to disengage or escalate?
- What information would help you make that call faster?

**5. SEL engagement (Kaleigh VanAlstine, multiple staff)**
- Describe a SEL lesson that went badly. What made it fail?
- What does meaningful SEL look like when it works?
- What would make students want to engage with social-emotional content?

**6. Communication tools (Chelse Conway)**
- What does it look like when a student struggles to communicate their needs?
- What do you wish you knew about each student's communication style before a lesson?
- What tools do you currently use for AAC support?

## First Place AZ Research Angles

**1. Daily living transitions**
- Walk me through a resident's typical morning. Where are the friction points?
- What does "successfully independent" look like for adults in your community?
- What workarounds do residents have built for daily tasks?

**2. Employment navigation**
- What are the most common barriers between a resident wanting to work and actually holding a job?
- What does a good work day vs. a bad work day look like for residents?

**3. Self-advocacy**
- How do residents communicate their needs to support staff?
- When does communication break down?

**4. Privacy sensitivity**
- What data would residents NOT want captured or stored?
- What would make residents trust an AI tool?
