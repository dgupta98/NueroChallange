# Principled Innovation (PI) — ASU Framework & What It Means for This Challenge

**Keynote speaker:** Tara Bartlett, Principled Innovation Fellow at Arizona State University  
**Session:** Presented at the Oct. 24 Opening Ceremony  
**Why this matters:** Judges score "Principled Innovation" as one of the 10 rubric criteria. Understanding this framework is critical to what will be accepted vs. rejected.

---

## What Is Principled Innovation? (Verbatim Definition)

> "Principled Innovation is the ability to imagine new concepts, catalyze ideas, and form new solutions guided by principles that create positive change for humanity. The question, 'We can innovate, but should we?' places values and ethical understandings at the core of the practice. Principled Innovation recognizes four interconnected character assets – moral, civic, intellectual, and performance – that are developed over one's lifetime through self-reflection, working with others, and learning through experiences."

**The central question this framework asks:**
> "We can innovate, but **should we?**"

## The 4 Interconnected PI Character Assets

These four assets are developed through self-reflection, working with others, and learning through experiences:

1. **Moral** — Ethics, doing what is right, considering impact on others
2. **Civic** — Responsibility to community, society, broader public good
3. **Intellectual** — Critical thinking, curiosity, knowledge-building, reasoning
4. **Performance** — Action, execution, follow-through, accountability

**Activity scenario used in the keynote:**
> "Your team has a great idea for an AI tool. It's innovative, technically feasible, and your team is confident you can build a strong prototype within two weeks. Then you meet with your Challenge Partner. The people they serve don't seem particularly interested in your idea. Instead, they describe a different problem — one that's less exciting technologically and may require your team to rethink much of what you've already planned. You have limited time and a competition to win. Do you keep building, modify your idea, or start over? What values should guide your decision?"

**What PI teaches about this scenario:**
> "PI doesn't necessarily give us the answer; it changes the quality of the questions we ask before acting."

## Final Thoughts from the PI Keynote (Verbatim)

> "Make the decision that feels like the right fit and take action. It's better to try a solution than be paralyzed by the fear of making a mistake. Afterwards, examine your choice… How did it go? What have I learned from this specific situation? What would I do differently next time? ## What PI Means for What Gets Built (Practical Implications)

### What IS Principled Innovation in an AI tool:

- AI that centers **human dignity and user agency** — the user can understand, control, and opt out
- Solutions grounded in **lived experience of actual users** (the Partner research requirement)
- Technology that **responds to real, researched needs** rather than assumed ones
- Building for **the most marginalized** rather than the average user
- Designs that are **accessible** across different communication, sensory, cognitive, and motor needs
- Honest about **bias, limits, and failure modes** of the AI
- Clear about **when the system should defer to a human** (educator, caregiver, professional)
- **Privacy-first** design — collect only what's necessary, protect what's collected

### What is NOT Principled Innovation (will hurt rubric scores):

- AI added for marketing/impressiveness rather than genuine utility
- Generic productivity tools that could serve anyone, not specifically neurodivergent users
- Solutions that make decisions FOR users without their input or understanding
- Tools that would require collecting unnecessary personal/sensitive data
- Systems that don't account for the wide variance in how neurodivergent users communicate or interact
- Ignoring what Challenge Partner research reveals and building the original idea anyway
- Code that is entirely AI-generated with no human understanding of it

## Rubric Criterion: Principled Innovation (Verbatim from Rubric)

**Evaluates:** How meaningfully and responsibly AI or advanced technology is integrated.

**Key Question:**
> "Was AI (or other advanced tech) applied in a way that enhances accessibility, personalization, or learning outcomes?"

**To score high on this criterion, your solution must demonstrate:**
- AI is **purposeful** (not decorative)
- AI enhances **accessibility, personalization, or learning outcomes** specifically
- Responsible handling of privacy, bias, accessibility, and user agency
- Evidence of ethical decision-making throughout the design process

## Responsible AI Checklist (From Handbook — Required Self-Check)

These are the exact questions judges will ask. Your team must be able to answer all of them:

| Question Area | What You Must Explain |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | The specific benefit AI enables and why a simpler approach is not enough. |
| **Agency** | How users understand, control, correct, dismiss, or opt out of system suggestions. |
| **Privacy** | What data is needed, what is NOT collected, and how sensitive information is protected. |
| **Bias and limits** | Where outputs may be incomplete or wrong and how the experience reduces harm. |
| **Accessibility** | How the interface supports varied communication, sensory, cognitive, and motor needs. |
| **Human support** | When the system should defer to a person, service, educator, or qualified professional. |

## How to Integrate PI Into Your Project Narrative

When pitching and in written responses, proactively address:

1. **Why we chose this problem** — grounded in partner research, not assumption
2. **Who benefits and who might be harmed** — specific, honest analysis
3. **What AI specifically enables** that no other approach could
4. **How users stay in control** of the AI's suggestions
5. **What data we collect and don't collect** and why
6. **Where our system might be wrong** and what guardrails we built
7. **When a human should take over** instead of the system
8. **How we designed for accessibility** across varied users

Teams that explicitly use PI language and demonstrate PI thinking in their pitch and written responses will score significantly higher on the Principled Innovation rubric criterion.
