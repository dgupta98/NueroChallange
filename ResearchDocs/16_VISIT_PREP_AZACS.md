## Who Is AZACS — The 60-Second Picture

Arizona Autism Charter Schools (AZACS) is Arizona's only public charter school network built specifically for students with autism and other neurodiversities. It is **tuition-free**, runs **K–12+ (to age 22)**, and serves **1,300+ current students** across 6 physical campuses + 2 online campuses. Every classroom is grounded in **Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA)** and run by specially trained staff.

**Why they're your hackathon partner:** They have 8 teachers and staff who've described specific, daily pain points — and those people have blocked calendar time to talk to you. Your job is to listen, understand, and design something that solves a real problem for them.

**Core tension:** Teachers are doing everything manually. The problems aren't new, but no tool has been good enough for neurodivergent-specific classrooms. AI could change that.

## Key Questions to Ask During Meetings

Use these as a guide — adapt based on who you're talking to:

**Understanding the problem:**
- Walk me through a typical [lesson / day / interaction]. Where does it break down?
- How long does [the manual task] take you currently?
- What have you tried before that didn't work, and why?
- Who else is affected when this goes wrong — just you, or students/parents too?

**Understanding the user:**
- How do your students prefer to receive information? (Visual, audio, written?)
- What does "engagement" look like in your classroom vs. disengagement?
- What's the biggest trigger for behavioral incidents?

**Understanding constraints:**
- What devices/tools do students and staff already have access to?
- What would make a new tool trustworthy vs. something you'd abandon after a week?
- What would you be nervous about handing off to AI?

**Understanding success:**
- If we solved this perfectly, what would your day look like differently?
- What does "independence" look like for a student who has achieved it?

## Neurodiversity — What You Need to Know Before These Meetings

You are not expected to be a clinician. But you will be talking to special education teachers who use clinical vocabulary daily. Not knowing what they mean will cost you credibility and understanding. Here is what you will encounter at AZACS specifically.

### Conditions You Will Encounter at AZACS

| Condition | What It Is | How It Shows Up in the Classroom |
|---|---|---|
| **Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)** | Neurodevelopmental condition affecting social communication, sensory processing, and behavior patterns. All AZACS students have autism or another neurodiversity. | Difficulty with transitions, unexpected changes, sensory overwhelm, literal language interpretation, repetitive behaviors (stimming), varied communication profiles |
| **ADHD** | Attention regulation disorder — not "can't focus" but inconsistent focus. 30–80% of autistic students also have ADHD. | Task initiation paralysis (starting is hard), time blindness, working memory limits, emotional dysregulation, hyperfocus on preferred topics |
| **Intellectual Disability (ID)** | Significant limits in both intellectual functioning and adaptive behavior. ~30–40% of autistic people have co-occurring ID. | Functional program students at AZACS. Lower literacy baseline, concrete thinking, needs repetition and visuals, daily living skills are explicit curriculum goals |
| **Dyslexia** | Reading disorder affecting phonological processing. Can co-occur with autism and ADHD. | Struggles with CKLA (the rigorous phonics curriculum Dylan Shloss described). Oral ability may far exceed written output. |
| **Sensory Processing Disorder (SPD)** | Brain difficulty organizing sensory input. Near-universal in autism. | Covering ears, avoiding certain textures, reacting to fluorescent lights, seeking movement/pressure. AZACS has sensory rooms for this reason. |
| **Anxiety** | Extremely common co-occurring condition with autism. | "Behavioral issues" during transitions, new people, changes to routine. Often what looks like defiance is anxiety. |

### The Three AZACS Program Tracks — Mapped to Support Needs

| Track | Students Served | What Staff Are Managing |
|---|---|---|
| **Academic** | Near grade-level academically; moderate autism support needs | Social communication, sensory, EF — curriculum is standard |
| **Modified Academic** | Scattered academic skills; moderate communication deficits | Everything above + adapted curriculum + more 1:1 time |
| **Functional Academic** | Significant ID + autism; limited communication | ABA-based clinical model; daily living skills as core curriculum; AAC often needed |

### Executive Function — Why This Is the Thread Through Everything

Every pain point from every AZACS teacher traces back to **executive function (EF)** — the brain's management system. EF covers: task initiation, working memory, emotional control, flexibility, planning, organization, time management, sustained attention, and impulse control.

Students with autism and/or ADHD have EF profiles that make these harder than for neurotypical students. This is why:
- Kaleigh's students won't self-track — requires task initiation + sustained effort
- Dylan's students can't get through CKLA — requires sustained attention + working memory
- Gave's students won't take responsibility — requires goal-directed persistence + self-monitoring
- Harpreet's students need different scaffolding in real time — sensory + EF both vary moment to moment

**If your solution addresses EF, it applies across all three program tracks.**

### What You MUST Know About Communication at AZACS

Not every AZACS student speaks verbally. Across the school you will encounter:
- **Fully verbal students** — may still have pragmatic (social) communication differences
- **Students who use scripts** — repeating memorized phrases; not the same as spontaneous communication
- **Students using AAC devices** — apps like Proloquo2Go, LAMP, TouchChat; address them directly
- **Non-speaking students** — may have full comprehension; do not talk around them

Chelse Conway's pain point is specifically about this: staff don't always know how each student communicates, creating friction every interaction.

### What Stimming Is — And Why You Don't Design It Away

Stimming (self-stimulatory behavior) = rocking, hand-flapping, humming, spinning, finger-flicking. It is **neurological self-regulation**, not disruptive behavior. Many autistic students stim more when stressed or excited. Suppressing it causes distress. Do not design solutions that try to eliminate or penalize stimming.

### What "Meltdown" Means — And How It's Different From a Tantrum

A **meltdown** is neurological overwhelm — the person has exceeded their capacity to self-regulate. It is not a tantrum (which is goal-directed). During a meltdown, the person cannot reason, respond logically, or de-escalate on command. The teacher's job is to reduce stimulation and wait. A **shutdown** is the same overwhelm directed inward — going silent, non-responsive, withdrawing.

Neither is a behavior problem to punish. Both are signals that the environment or demands exceeded the person's current capacity.

### Terms You Will Hear — Know These Cold

| Term | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **ABA** | Applied Behavior Analysis — the evidence-based teaching method used at AZACS. Tracks behavior, antecedents, consequences. |
| **IEP** | Individualized Education Program — each student's legal document outlining their goals, services, and accommodations |
| **BCBA** | Board Certified Behavior Analyst — designs the behavioral strategy for each student |
| **Prompt / Prompt fading** | A cue that helps someone do something; fading means reducing that support toward independence |
| **Reinforcement** | Anything that increases a behavior — central to ABA instruction |
| **AAC** | Augmentative and Alternative Communication — picture boards, devices, apps |
| **Echolalia** | Repeating heard phrases — a communication and comfort mechanism |
| **Dysregulation** | Emotional/behavioral state where self-control is exceeded |
| **Antecedent** | The trigger before a behavior — key to ABA analysis |
| **Masking** | Hiding neurodivergent traits to appear neurotypical — exhausting; associated with burnout |
| **Neurodivergent / Neurotypical** | Brain processes differently from average / Brain processes in the typical way |

## What a Strong AZACS Solution Looks Like

A solution AZACS staff would actually use:
- Saves the teacher at least 30 min/day of prep work, OR
- Shifts responsibility to the student (self-tracking, self-advocacy), OR
- Helps the teacher see quickly what each student needs in real time

Red flags — things that will NOT work:
- Generic "AI lesson generator" with no autism-specific design
- Requires students to input data themselves without built-in scaffolding
- Assumes internet access or devices not already in classrooms
- Looks/feels like a consumer app (students are neurodivergent — design matters)
- Ignores the IEP framework (solutions must work within existing compliance structure)
