Josh's direct answer to "what would you specifically want changed right now for residents":

> *"If I come to First Place and I know that I… is there something that would allow me to connect with my support therapist to say, like, cool, I met that skill? Awesome. ## Key Insights — Organized by Theme

### 1. The Engagement Timeline (Validates Our Hypothesis)

- First 2–3 months: residents are overwhelmed, often retreating to their apartments ("lull")
- Lease is typical Phoenix lease — family cannot stay longer than 14 days in a calendar year. Getting away from parents is by design.
- At the 3-month mark: staff have a formal conversation — if someone is not integrating, why? Figure it out fast because at $4,400/month, this needs to work.
- **Quote:** "Once people do [integrate], they're more likely to want to plan for a job. They're more likely to take care of their house. Why? Because you're gonna have friends over."
- Social connection is the **keystone that unlocks everything else** (employment, independence, hygiene, cooking). Confirmed explicitly.

### 2. The Activity App Is Called MyClip (Not LifeLoop)

- Every other Saturday: new community life activities posted on **MyClip** (mobile + laptop app)
- Residents sign up like a gym ("I want aerobics, yoga, and meditation on this given day")
- Sign-up creates accountability — residents must notify staff if they can't make it so someone else can jump in line
- **Every activity has intentional skill building attached** — even fun ones. Cooking class = social skills, schedule adherence, responsibility, communication.
- Note: Our pre-visit research identified LifeLoop. The tour guide called it MyClip. These may be the same product with a different local name, or different systems. Needs clarification.

### 3. What First Place Does NOT Do (Confirmed Boundaries)

- Not a medical home, not assisted care living
- Cannot support severe behavioral health crises (self-injury, property destruction, aggression >20% of time)
- Does not administer medication — they track adherence only
- Medication management approach: partnered with **Craiglin** (pill-dispensing mechanism), or EF skill building around refill timing ("you have 7 days left, you need 2 weeks to get a refill")
- **Quote:** "We're not equipped to support people with severe behavioral health crises."

### 4. Architecture Is Purpose-Built for Neurodiversity

Details confirmed in person:
- **No fluorescent lights** — all low-voltage LED. Explicitly for visual sensory sensitivity.
- **Sound dampening** throughout — white noise reduces ambient noise in common areas
- **Extra-wide hallways** — mobility AND sensory: people who dislike close proximity have more space
- **Recessed apartment doorways** — 2-foot buffer before entering public hall; can hear people coming; eases the transition from private to public
- **Wayfinding by floor color**: 4th floor = dark blue, 3rd floor = seafoam green, etc. Neurologically meaningful color coding, not just aesthetic
- **Sensory zones by floor**:
  - 4th floor: quietest, darkest, for sensory-sensitive residents (also the coders and gamers / introverts)
  - 3rd floor: brighter, more social, musicians, extroverts
  - 2nd floor: middle tone
- **Key/wallet drop zone** by apartment door — explicit ADHD accommodation ("folks know they don't have to scramble around their apartment looking for keys, wallet, glasses")
- **Dull floors and countertops** — reduces breakage for residents who are clumsy or new to independent living

### 5. The iGuard Stove System (Smart Home Reality Check)

- Every apartment has an iGuard: detects lack of motion over a set time period, auto-shuts off the stove
- **15,000 activations since 2018 opening** — "one out of those 15,000 could have meant the end of our building"
- **Limitation revealed**: Cannot be disabled for long cooks. If a resident is cooking something for 2 hours, iGuard will shut off the stove. Most residents don't cook meals longer than 30 minutes (pizza, quick meals) so this works in practice.
- Explicitly mentioned as a model: "if you incorporate this in any built environment, it can be really successful — think about how you do this in a work setting where accommodations are not robust"

### 6. Alexa in Every Apartment

- Baseline EF support tool: alarms, reminders, ordering groceries, ordering medication refills
- Staff sometimes build on this — teaching residents to use the Whole Foods website for grocery ordering as a skill-building exercise
- Simple, cheap, works

### 7. The Wearable Failure — Critical for Our Research

- First Place trialed an **Amazon AWS wearable device** for tracking physiological symptoms to predict seizure activity
- **Why it failed:** Sensory issues. Residents did not want to wear it. Material was uncomfortable. Universal rejection.
- **Direct lesson:** Even great technology fails if the hardware creates a sensory barrier to entry.
- Discussion that followed: ring vs. wristband vs. glasses as alternatives. Josh mentioned **meta glasses** as a concept he's personally interested in.
- **Quote from Josh:** "If you had meta glasses that were detecting a person's facial expressions, and being able to inform you in your ear — if someone has a really hard time with differentiating facial expressions… imagine glasses that would say 'this person seems uncomfortable, stop perseverating on this topic.' That'd be huge."
- Also mentioned: plain language converter in glasses for ballot language / complex documents — especially relevant for intellectual disability

### 8. Security Model

- 24/7 concierge monitoring cameras (everywhere except inside apartments — "violation of acts and policies and nor do we want that")
- Cannot enter the building or any floor without a badge
- **No curfew** — dignity of risk fully implemented. Resident can go to Circle K at 2am. Staff will ask if it's a good idea. If yes, they'll note it and check back after a reasonable time.
- Law enforcement awareness: Maricopa County Sheriff's Office, Goodyear PD have done autism awareness trainings at First Place. They know the building. Some officers are dispatched who have specific autism training.

### 9. Employment and Skills Infrastructure

- Three departments: Support Staff / Community Life / Employment
- Support staff take data on skill development, but no resident-facing record of it
- **No set curriculum** on the support side (clinical/TA has curriculum via SARRC). Residents direct their own learning goals.
- **Precision Inc.** (led by Ernie Dionasis): entire software company built to hire neurodivergent employees. "Pretty sure every single one of their employees has a TBI." Mentioned as employer partner.

### 10. VR Room — Future Plans

- 4th floor corner currently empty — conversations about converting to a VR room
- Use cases mentioned: employment scenarios, going on a date, interview practice, interacting with law enforcement
- **Vertra**: system used by sister nonprofit specifically for law enforcement interaction simulation
- Josh was enthusiastic about this but framed it as "fingers crossed"

### 11. Community Definition — A Nuanced Point

- Josh explicitly pushed back on neurotypical definitions of community
- Resident who spends all day on Discord talking with friends in Japan = valid community if they report they're happy
- "We know when it gets unhealthy, for sure. But we never want to impose what matters to us as a neurotypical world on them."
- Families complain about the game room ("all my son does is play video games") — Josh's response: if they're out of their apartment and playing with someone instead of alone, "we win"

### 12. Foundation for Growing Children

- ADHD + autism co-occurring: "1st cousins — really common co-occurring condition"
- Partnership with Foundation for Growing Children — 2 residents currently connected to this foundation living at First Place
- Building being evaluated for adaptation for this population

## What This Changes About Our Solution Direction

### Before This Visit
We were focused on: social connection scaffold built on top of shared activity history (LifeLoop/MyClip data).

### After This Visit
The social connection angle is still valid — Josh confirmed it as the keystone ("once they have friends, they want a job, they clean their house"). But Josh gave us something more specific:

**The skill tracker is an unmet need with a direct ask, no existing product, and AI makes it meaningfully better than paper.**

How these connect:
- Resident tracks skill milestones (cooking, money management, relationships)
- AI suggests next skills based on current progress
- Social milestone: "you've attended 3 cooking classes — you're ready to host a cooking night with a neighbor"
- This bridges skill development → social connection → employment readiness in one flow

### The Pitch Frame That Emerged From This Visit

> "First Place has 1,600 activities, a 24/7 concierge, and staff who take data on resident progress. What they don't have is a way for the resident to see their own growth. Josh told us directly: 'If I came to First Place and could track how much I'm improving in certain skills, like I would want that.' We built that — a resident-owned skill progress tool that uses AI to suggest next steps and connect skill milestones to social and employment readiness."
