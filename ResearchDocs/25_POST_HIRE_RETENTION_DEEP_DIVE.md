## Why Autistic Adults Lose Jobs — The Research

### Peer-Reviewed Study: Employer-Initiated Terminations
**Source:** "Perspectives on Employer-Initiated Terminations Among Young Adults on the Autism Spectrum," *PMC9838300* (2023, National Library of Medicine)

**Method:** Surveyed autistic young adults who had been fired; coded their self-reported reasons.

**Results — top categories:**

| Reason Category | Frequency | Examples from Participants |
|---|---|---|
| **Work performance** | Most common | Task execution issues, quality standards |
| **Social difficulties** | **16%** | "Lack of communication skills"; "Failing to recognize social cues"; "I apparently talked down to my supervisor when I just had a question — I made him cry and was fired"; "A customer yelled at me and I started crying and couldn't stop, so I was let go" |
| **Attendance** | Significant | Difficulty with punctuality, calling out, consistency |
| **Mental health challenges** | Significant | Burnout, anxiety, overwhelm |
| Autism cited directly | 5% | Explicitly named autism as the reason |
| Discrimination | 5% | Employer misconduct |

**Critical finding #1:** The challenges were present **early** in employment tenure, not decline over time. Most job losses happen in the first weeks or months — i.e., during the probationary period.

**Critical finding #2:** Nearly half of participants attributed their termination to **internal causes** — "my personality and weirdness," "I was not on my best behavior." This internalized blame compounds isolation and discourages re-employment attempts.

**Critical finding #3 (Buckland Review, UK Government, 2023):** The causes are NOT all individual — workplace structure is co-responsible:
- 1 in 3 autistic employees felt unable to discuss adjustment needs at all
- Over 1/4 who requested adjustments were refused
- Over 1 in 10 found their adjustment was poorly implemented
- 65% of autistic employees hide their autism at work (fear of discrimination)

## The 90-Day Cliff — How Support Falls Off

### Government Job Coaching Structure
- **Vocational Rehabilitation (VR) and WIOA-funded job coaches** work with clients for **up to 90 days post-hire**
- This is the standard across most US states under federal funding guidelines
- The job coach's role: on-the-job training support, employer communication, accommodation advocacy

### The Mismatch
- **Many companies' probationary periods EXCEED 90 days** — 3-6 months is common; some extend to 12 months
- When the job coach exits at day 90, the support falls off — but the highest-risk period (proving yourself, passing probation) often extends well beyond that
- **Source:** Heather Dicks, career development advisor, Georgia Tech EXCEL program, 20 years experience: *"I could see these models evolving to being supportive at those critical junctures of the probationary period being over, or the one-year job review, or the annual evaluation that everyone dreads."*

### The Individual Placement and Support (IPS) Model
**IPS** is the gold-standard evidence-based supported employment model:
- Job coaches stay involved post-hire, check in regularly, intervene at early warning signs
- Produces the **highest sustained competitive employment rates** for autistic adults of any model tested
- Currently under a new randomized controlled trial specifically for autism+IDD (2025-2026 trial, registered at ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06829264)

**Why IPS doesn't solve it for First Place:** IPS requires a funded job coach, ongoing employer relationships, and clinical infrastructure. It's a service delivery model — not scalable to the 70K+ who age out annually without access to this level of support. First Place has employment staff, but they cannot maintain active IPS-level engagement with every employed resident indefinitely.

## What Currently Exists for Post-Hire Support

### 1. Job Accommodation Network (JAN) — askjan.org
- **What it is:** Free EEOC-funded advisory service (phone/chat/database) helping workers identify and request workplace accommodations
- **What it does:** Search by disability and job function → get accommodation suggestions → guidance on how to request
- **Format:** Database + consultation. Not an app. Not real-time. Requires user to know they need help and navigate a website.
- **Gap:** No AI. No personalization. No real-time situation support. Requires significant executive function to use.
- **Relevance:** This is the best public resource for accommodation navigation. Your product should do what JAN does but automatically, conversationally, and in-the-moment.

### 2. Inclusively "Retain" — inclusively.com
- **What it is:** Workforce intelligence platform — "real-time insight for smarter workforce decisions"
- **What it does:** Helps employees find and use company benefits + support; Retain Navigator and Retain Advisor embed in Teams/Slack/ServiceNow
- **Who it's for:** General workforce (not ND-specific). Employer-side analytics. HR departments.
- **Gap:** Not autism-specific. Not coaching. Benefits navigation, not workplace situation support. HR/employer tool, not worker tool.

### 3. Dynseo
- Job coaching data tools, digital support for job coaches managing clients
- Staff-facing. The coach uses it, not the worker.

### 4. EZducate AI
- Workplace social scripts, task analysis builders, ADA accommodation templates
- More of a therapist/job coach productivity tool than something a worker uses independently

### 5. auticon Embedded Job Coaches
- Works: auticon employs autistic professionals and provides job coaches as part of their employment model
- **Doesn't scale:** Only helps the people auticon directly employs (primarily senior IT consultants). AMC, Zoo, Aramark employees have nothing comparable.

### 6. SAP/Microsoft Ongoing Mentors
- Works within those companies only
- Built-in infrastructure that other employers can't replicate without significant investment

### 7. Georgia Tech LLM Job Coach (NSF $500K, Jan 2026)
- Jennifer Kim + Mark Riedl + Heather Dicks
- Current focus: interview prep, resume, cover letters, rejection handling, accommodation requests
- **Explicitly identified post-hire as the next gap** but hasn't built it yet
- Status: Still in research/development phase. Not a product.
- **This is your most direct academic validation.** Georgia Tech named the exact gap. Nobody has shipped it.

## The Specific Scenarios That Have No Solution

These are real situations autistic workers face, confirmed by the termination research and First Place's own data. Zero products address them in real time:

### Scenario 1: Ambiguous Feedback
> "My manager said my performance was 'not quite what we expected.' I don't know if this means I'm about to be fired or it's fine. I don't know how to respond."

**Why it breaks down:** Neurotypical managers communicate evaluative feedback obliquely. Autistic workers often need explicit, literal communication. The interpretation gap is a real termination risk.

**What would help:** Translate the message → give probability estimates of what it means → suggest 2-3 concrete responses → offer language for a clarifying conversation with the manager.

### Scenario 2: Schedule or Rule Change
> "They changed my shift without telling me. I'm standing here and I don't know what to do. I feel like I'm going to melt down."

**Why it breaks down:** Autistic adults often rely on predictable routines as a coping structure. Unexpected changes trigger dysregulation that can manifest as visible distress — which gets misread as behavioral problems.

**What would help:** Immediate grounding response → step-by-step what to do right now → later: how to request written advance notice of schedule changes as a formal accommodation.

### Scenario 3: Social Conflict / Misread Interaction
> "I think I upset a coworker today but I don't know what I did. They stopped talking to me. I don't know if I should say something or leave it alone."

**Why it breaks down:** Social misreads accumulate into workplace relationship failures. Many terminations coded as "work performance" actually originate here.

**What would help:** Walk through what happened → interpret likely meaning of coworker's behavior → give concrete options for repair if needed → provide a script if the person wants to address it.

### Scenario 4: Probationary Review Coming
> "I have my 90-day review tomorrow and nobody has told me what to expect. I don't know if I'm doing well or not. I'm terrified."

**Why it breaks down:** Reviews require self-advocacy, interpreting ongoing feedback, and communicating needs. Preparation requires executive function that may not be available under stress.

**What would help:** Explain what a 90-day review typically covers → help gather and organize examples of good work → prepare for likely questions → provide scripts for raising accommodation needs in a review context.

### Scenario 5: Accommodation Request
> "I know I work better with written instructions instead of verbal ones but I don't know how to ask for that without making it weird."

**Why it breaks down:** 75% of autistic workers receive no adjustments. The barrier is not employer unwillingness alone — many workers don't know their rights, don't know what to ask for, or fear asking will harm them.

**What would help:** Identify what accommodation would help → explain the legal right (ADA, reasonable adjustment) → draft the request in accessible language → prepare for the conversation.

### Scenario 6: Post-Shift Decompression
> "That shift was really hard. I don't even know why. I just need to process it."

**Why it breaks down:** Autistic adults often experience delayed emotional processing. Unprocessed workplace stress accumulates into burnout, absenteeism, and eventually job loss — often without the person or employer recognizing the pattern.

**What would help:** A low-pressure reflection prompt → identify the specific stressor → name what kind of support would help → optionally connect to a First Place staff member or community life event.

## The Assistive Technology Research Gap — Peer Reviewed Confirmation

**Source:** Zhou, Richard & Kim (2025), "Employment-related assistive technologies for autistic individuals: A scoping review," *Journal of Vocational Rehabilitation, 62(2), 191–206.*

**Key findings:**
- 30 studies reviewed covering all AT for autism employment (1998–2022)
- **Primary focus: pre-employment** — video modeling, virtual reality, covert audio coaching
- **Few studies addressed on-the-job performance or retention**
- Most ATs were applied passively with limited user training or engagement
- Researchers from special education and psychology dominated; minimal rehabilitation counseling involvement

**Direct quote from abstract:** *"While ATs show promise in pre-employment settings, their use for post-hiring support remains underexplored. Future research should prioritize active, post-hire tools."*

**This is peer-reviewed confirmation of the exact gap you're building for.** Published January 2025. This is your scientific citation.

## The Accommodation Gap — Numbers That Matter

From the **Cureus systematic review on Workplace Accommodations and Employment Outcomes (2025)**:

- **75% of autistic workers receive NO workplace adjustments** — despite legal entitlement under ADA (US) / Equality Act (UK)
- When accommodations ARE provided: **3.14× higher odds of appropriate employment** (Harvey et al., 2020)
- Disclosure barrier: 65% of autistic employees hide their autism, mostly fear of discrimination
- 1 in 3 feel unable to discuss adjustment needs at all
- Of those who do request: 25%+ refused; 10%+ got poorly implemented adjustments

**The math:** 75% don't get adjustments. Adjustments triple employment odds. This is a massive, documented, preventable loss.

**JAN (Job Accommodation Network) is the public solution** — but it requires a person to: (1) know the resource exists, (2) know what to search for, (3) navigate a website, (4) translate generic information to their situation, (5) write a formal request. Every one of these steps is an executive function load on exactly the population least equipped to manage it.

An AI that does this conversationally and in-the-moment removes every barrier except the will to ask.

## What's Possible — The Product Design Space

### Tier 1: Buildable in 10 Days (MVP Layer 2)

A well-prompted LLM with a clean mobile-first UX covering:

**A. Situation Decoder**
- Input: "My manager said X" or pre-built scenario buttons
- Output: Most likely interpretation + 2-3 concrete response options + what NOT to do
- Tuned to First Place's actual employer partners (AMC scripts, Zoo scripts, Aramark scripts)

**B. Accommodation Navigator**
- Input: "I struggle with [X] at work"
- Output: What accommodation would help → your legal right under ADA → draft request language → how to bring it up
- Draws from JAN database logic but conversational

**C. Review Prep Coach**
- Pre-built modules for 30-day, 90-day, 6-month, annual reviews
- Helps gather examples, anticipate questions, prepare responses
- "What did you do well this month?" → helps them articulate it

**D. Post-Shift Processing**
- Simple mood + situation log after work
- AI names the pattern ("You had three rough Tuesdays — that's when the manager does floor reviews")
- Optionally surfaces a relevant community life event or First Place support contact

### Tier 2: Near-Term Enhancement (post-hackathon)

**E. Pattern Recognition Across Shifts**
- After 4-6 weeks of data, identify recurring triggers
- "You consistently log stress on days with shift changes. Here's an accommodation request template."

**F. Manager Communication Bridge**
- With employer permission: pre-formatted structured feedback requests that workers can send to managers (ADA-compliant, non-threatening)
- Bridges the explicit communication style autistic workers need

**G. Skills → Job Readiness → Job Connection**
- The skill tracker (Layer 1) feeds into the retention coach (Layer 2)
- "You've tracked 'handling unexpected changes' as a skill for 3 months — you're ready to try a job that involves shift work"

### Tier 3: Long-Term Vision (pitch the future state)

**H. Employer Dashboard (Opt-In, Aggregate)**
- Employer sees: "3 of 5 employees at this location logged stress around shift change communication"
- Not individual data — aggregate signal that prompts employer to improve processes
- Closes the loop from individual accommodation to systemic workplace change

**I. Integration with Supported Employment Funders**
- IPS case managers get a data feed (with consent) that extends their 90-day view
- Government VR funding could potentially pay for the app as a continuation of job coaching
