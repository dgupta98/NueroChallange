**Author:** Naman Dewan · **Date:** Oct 30, 2026

## Evidence-based practices we build on (verified)

The 2020 report from the National Clearinghouse on Autism Evidence and Practice (NCAEP), UNC Frank Porter Graham Institute, reviewed studies from 1990–2017 and lists **28 evidence-based practices** for autistic children, youth and young adults. Step Cards combines four of them:

| Practice (NCAEP 2020) | Where it shows up in Step Cards |
|---|---|
| **Task Analysis**: breaking a skill into small, ordered steps | AI turns one instruction into step cards |
| **Visual Supports**: pictures, written words and objects that support a skill | Pictogram cards on the tablet |
| **Prompting**: giving help, then **fading** it as the learner succeeds | Cards shrink as the student masters a routine |
| **Technology-Aided Instruction & Intervention** | Delivered on a tablet |

Two more on the list fit the home and later stages: **Parent-Implemented Intervention** (the cards go home) and **Self-Management** (students check off their own steps).

Sources: [NCAEP 2020 EBP report](https://ncaep.fpg.unc.edu/?p=395); [the list of 28 EBPs, AFIRM / NPDC](https://autismpdc.fpg.unc.edu/ebps/)

Note for Q&A: some researchers criticise the review's inclusion rules (Leaf et al., 2021, *check first*). Our answer: we use these practices as design principles, and we'll measure our own results with the partner.

## Working memory and multi-step directions

- When working memory is overloaded, students lose track of multi-step instructions and can look inattentive when the real problem is holding too much at once. Recommended supports: **one step at a time, numbered steps, and visuals that back up spoken directions**, building from short sequences to longer ones. Source: [ODU Training & Technical Assistance Center, "Executive functioning skills: working memory and autism" (Part II)](https://ttac.odu.edu/autism/executive-functioning-skills-working-memory-and-autism-part-ii/) (practitioner guidance, not a study).
- Studies summarised second-hand suggest autistic teens can hold **less visual information at once**, so **fewer icons per card** is better. *Check first.* Design rule we take from it: one action per card, and at most one pictogram per card.
- Acting out instructions helped some autistic children remember them (Wang et al. 2022, mixed results in Xie et al. 2024). *Check first.* This supports cards for **doing** steps, not just reading them.

## Carrying skills from school to home (generalisation)

- In a study of 248 autistic children, skills **generalised less well from school to home** than from home to school (PACT-G trial analysis, University of Manchester). *Check first: confirm the direction of the finding in the paper before quoting it.* Source: [Manchester research portal](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/generalisation-of-social-communication-skills-by-autistic-childre/)
- Teaching the same skill in **more than one setting** improved generalisation to home (older, small study from 1980; use only as background).
- What this means for us: the same cards used at school **and** at home should help the skill transfer. That's the "cards go home" feature.

## Design principles carried over from the team's Oct 28 research

From the talk by psychiatrist Dr. Sasha Hamdani on ADHD and executive function (team notes, Oct 28):
- **Externalise working memory:** the cards hold the steps.
- **Engineer the start:** the first card is always tiny.
- **Diffuse shame:** no red marks, no "you failed," and "help me" is private. *This is the top rule.*
- **Low-effort input:** the teacher just speaks, and students only tap.

From First Place AZ: ADHD and autism often co-occur, so design for both. An earlier wearable trial failed because of sensory discomfort, so Step Cards stays app-only.

## Background facts for the pitch (check first)

- Special-education teachers spend a large share of their time on paperwork. A Frontline Education blog cites "up to 50%," but that figure comes from older studies. *Check before quoting.*
- UNC's **AFIRM-AI** project (2026–2029) is building a teacher tool that matches IEP goals to evidence-based practices. It's still in research, so it isn't a competitor, but it shows the field is moving this way.

## Existing tools and how we differ


Judges will ask "doesn't X already do this?" Here's the answer for each.

| Tool | What it does | What it doesn't do |
|---|---|---|
| **Goblin Tools** (Magic ToDo) | Free web/app tool. Type a task and AI breaks it into smaller steps, with adjustable detail. Built for neurodivergent users. | Text only, built for one person typing their own tasks. No teacher, no classroom, no pictograms, no fading, no progress loop. |
| **Tiimo** | Visual daily planner for autistic and ADHD users with colour-coded timelines and icons. AI breaks goals into steps. Paid subscription. | A personal planner for routines planned ahead. Not live classroom instructions, and no teacher or parent loop. |
| **Goally** | Kids' tablet and apps: visual schedules, task analyses, token boards, behaviour logging. | Adults build every schedule and task analysis by hand, ahead of time. It can't handle an instruction the teacher says on the spot. |
| **Choiceworks** and other visual-schedule apps | Picture schedules, first-then boards, timers. | Manual setup, no AI, no personalisation per student. |
| **General teacher AI tools** (worksheet and lesson generators) | Make materials for the teacher. | Staff-facing documents, not live support for the student during the task. |

Sources: [Goblin Tools on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6739871366); [Warwick Library on Magic ToDo](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/library/using/productivity-tools/magic-todo-gobin-tools); [Tiimo overview](https://www.toolmage.com/en/tool/tiimo/); [Goally on Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/goally); [visual schedule apps list, Advocate Health](https://adscresources.advocatehealth.com/resources/visual-schedule-apps/)

### Our answer in one sentence

> Visual-schedule apps need an adult to build every schedule ahead of time, and AI step-breakers are text tools for one person. Step Cards takes the instruction a teacher says live, makes a version for each student, fades the help as they master it, and tells the teacher who's stuck.

### Partner tools to know about

- **MyClip** (First Place AZ's activity scheduling app). Open question: is it the same as LifeLoop? Step Cards wouldn't replace it. Later it could read the activity list, e.g. "cooking class at 5" → recipe cards.
- **AZACS's daily parent journal** (paper). We feed it rather than replace it.
